A century of bubbles, in one clean collection.

Refreshments is a concept showcase — a study in how a single design language can frame a family of very different drinks. Each product here belongs to The Coca-Cola Company, one of the world's most recognisable beverage portfolios.

The idea

Strip away the noise. Keep one accent, plenty of white space, and let each drink speak for itself.

The collection spans carbonated classics and real fruit juice — from the original Coca-Cola of 1886 to Minute Maid's orange juice. They share little in flavour, but they share a lineage, and a single, instantly familiar colour.

This site reimagines that lineage in Apple's minimalist language: an off-white canvas, one restrained red accent, frosted glass and a grid of quiet product cards. No clutter, no noise — just the drinks.

The collection

Five drinks, one colour — from 1886 to today.

A short timeline

1886

Coca-Cola is first served in Atlanta — the start of the family.

1940

Fanta arrives in Europe, bright and fruity.

1945

Minute Maid brings real orange juice to the table.

1961

Sprite joins with a clean, caffeine-free lemon-lime.

2005

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar proves great taste needs no sugar.

Today

The portfolio keeps growing — collected here in one clean lineup.

How it was built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript — nothing that needs a server.

Every page is hand-written HTML with a single stylesheet and one small script. The product grid, the detail dialog and the "Find your drink" quiz are all vanilla JavaScript; the home page's scroll effects use one small library, GSAP. No framework, no build step, no database.

Product imagery is a set of concept renders made for this study. The five drinks and their names belong to The Coca-Cola Company; this site is an unaffiliated student project.
